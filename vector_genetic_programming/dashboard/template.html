No evidence of skill

A genetic program searched 50,455 trading strategies across 21 crypto assets. To find out whether it discovered anything, the identical pipeline was re-run on 99 surrogate datasets built to contain no signal at all. This is the comparison.

The search performs worse than signal-free data p = 0.700

On the statistic that matters for a deployable strategy — the typical window, not the luckiest one — the real data produced an out-of-sample Sharpe of −0.533 against a null median of +0.255. Seventy percent of datasets with no signal in them did better.

Where the result falls in the null distribution

Each histogram is 99 runs of the same search on surrogate data that preserves volatility, fat tails and cross-asset correlation but destroys any predictable ordering. An edge would sit out in the right tail. None of these do.

Read the bottom-right panel. The other three ask whether the best of many tries looks lucky; that question flatters a search. The typical window asks whether an average period beats chance, and it is the only one that corresponds to money.

Two statistics disagree, and one of them is blind

Deflated Sharpe Ratio

0.970

Clears the conventional 0.95 bar. Read alone, it certifies this result. DSR corrects for selection across trials: it asks whether the best of 38,774 tries beats what the spread of those tries predicts.

Null control

p = 0.700

Rejects it. The null control re-runs the whole pipeline on data with no signal, so it catches bias that shifts every trial together — which leaves the cross-sectional spread untouched and DSR unmoved.

Both numbers are arithmetically correct. Eleven measurement defects were found in this project, and seven of them lived in exactly that blind spot: a lookahead feature, a decorrelated surrogate, nested out-of-sample windows, a survivor-biased universe, a ranking sentinel reported as performance, unequal numbers of tries in the max statistic, and results that changed between processes because a crossover operator depended on memory addresses. DSR saw none of them. Where the two disagree, believe the null control.

The information is real and it does not convert. One-day cross-sectional reversal genuinely predicts returns, but rebalancing daily costs 49% a year at 10 bps, and the gross Sharpe was already inside one standard deviation of the null. Breaking even would need round-trip execution under about 1.2 bps. An information coefficient that beats the null is necessary, not sufficient.

An earlier version of this diagnostic reported five features clearing a Bonferroni threshold with stable split-half signs. That was measured on raw cross-sectional IC tested against zero, and signal-free surrogates reproduce 92–124% of it — it described which assets are persistently volatile, not what happens next. Split-half stability could not have disagreed, because a static property is stable across halves by construction.
